Our Beginning

A Journey of Faith and Fellowship

Progressive thought draws its vitality from the liberation struggles of oppressed communities. For this reason, the history of the Church and the history of society complement one another. The bloodstains scattered along the pathways where our ancestors walked, the clinking of chains on their feet, the shroud of lonely darkness that engulfed them, and the sheer heights of their hardships endure as unforgettable, eternal truths. During the era when Brahmanical dominance was at its absolute peak, forms of punishment were barbaric and grotesque. Untouchability was believed to spread through the air. The right to wear clothing above the waist was denied. Even when meager clothing was permitted, the rule dictated that it had to be soiled with mud. Mothers and sisters had no right to cover their upper bodies. Living was permitted only in huts made of uncut leaves and grass without mud walls. False cases, destruction of land deeds to extinguish property rights, and eviction from homes without issuing receipts even after taxes were paid were commonplace. Not doing *Oozhiyam* (unpaid forced labor) or refusing to surrender women met with beastly torture. Having endured unimaginable atrocities, including murder, that shook human conscience, even the very psyche of our ancestors underwent deep alterations. The primitive frenzy nurtured by collective force and selfishness was the driving power of society back then. Later, this evolved into a parade of casteism. Castes eventually danced violently like Kathakali villains (*Kathi Vesham*). While all other societies walked forward, they forced us backward. In this period, a section lived as parasites, consuming and breathing the accumulated refuse and primitivism. They fragmented others, obliterated their identities, and plunged them into the quagmire. In this context, the sublime struggles waged by a small group of people in a tiny princely state against the primitive forces—who greedily hoarded millennia of decadence, brooded over them, multiplied, and celebrated life by infiltrating within—assume immense significance. The arrival of the British and the protection received through Christian missionaries and teachers led them toward a new stream of thought. The eras, events, experiences, and knowledge they passed through transformed into an awakening song. The role played by Christian missionaries in uprooting ideologies that were preserved within fortresses built of mud mixed with human blood—intended to remain unchanged until the end of the world—was monumental. Embracing the missionaries as a refuge to lay down their heavy burdens and suffering, a large section of the population converted to Christianity. The first person in Travancore to convert to the Protestant faith was Veda Manickam Maharasan. A devout Hindu believer, he met LMS missionary William Tobias Ringeltaube and embraced the Christian faith. The first Protestant church in Travancore was consecrated in 1804 at Mylaudy. Charles Mead was the successor to Rev. Ringeltaube. Setting up his mission headquarters at Nagercoil as per the instructions of Colonel Munro, Charles Mead established a printing press, English medium schools, and churches there. Thus, the arrival of the missionaries marked a new beginning in the social history of Travancore. The history of our church begins toward the end of the 1920s. Lazarus Memorial CSI Church, Aruvikkara, is a parish with a proud legacy of 101 years in the South Kerala Diocese of the Church of South India. This church is situated in Aruvikkara Grama Panchayat, Nedumangad Taluk, Thiruvananthapuram District. Comprising around 300 families, the history of this parish is intertwined with the liberation struggle of an oppressed community. It is no easy task to retrieve the historical facts of such an extensive era and capture them on paper. However, many details that were missed while compiling the 75-year history for the Platinum Jubilee Souvenir have now been incorporated into this report on the basis of church records. The notebook of memories preserved by elder parents who witnessed the inception of the church was helpful in writing that history. The subsequent history has been prepared in association with the Church Committee, grounded in official church records. A dewy, mist-clad morning in December 1920. Sri. Daniel Asan (known as Deekhanasan) and Kalkuzhi Sri. Daniel Vaidyan were discussing the need to find a place to worship God. They resolved to fight against the superstitions and social evils surrounding them. Realizing that the oppressed masses could find liberation only if the divine message of Christ reached human hearts, they— *(Daniel Asan [Deekhanasan], Lazarus)* —deliberated on preliminary plans to establish a congregation. During that time, Sri. Lazarus Contractor was a respected, educated, and socially active figure in the community. Sharing a close bond of affection with him, these individuals invited him to establish the church, an invitation he joyfully accepted. Centered on a place called Peruvarambu, they initiated an evangelistic outreach. Alongside them, Sri. Israel of Pulikkuzhi, and Sri. Kochan’s sons—Sri. Pathrose, Sri. John, Sri. Yesuvadiyan, and Sri. Samuel—began working in close collaboration. It was an era when caste-based untouchability and distance-pollution prevailed. The role played by this small group in resisting upper-caste hegemony was unparalleled. Armed with traditional Ayurveda, martial arts, and unified solidarity, the upper-caste establishment feared confronting them directly. As a result of their efforts, members of the Nadar community who were Hindu believers embraced the Christian path. A thatched grass shed owned by Sri. Israel at Pulikkuzhi was the first place obtained for their worship. Subsequently, worship was conducted in a shed constructed using wild tree branches in Sri. Vedakkannu's newly reclaimed land (*puthuval*) at Kooramkunnu. During this era, leaders including Sri. Perumal Vadyar, Gnanaprakasam Prasangiyar, Muthayya Prasangiyar, Mailamoodu Vedanayakam, Karikkuzhi Jacob, Joel, Israel, Pathiriyodu Daniel, Charuvuvila Thompson, Daniel Desakaval, Kakkodu Yohannan, Enos Prasangiyar, Parakkonam Nallathambi, Velloorkonam Johny, Parakkonam Muthunayakam, Kochan Manuel, Kalkuzhi Jacob, Parakkonam Barnabas, Kochan Jacob, and others fulfilled their mission through evangelism and participating in worship services. However, when government permission could not be obtained to construct a permanent house of worship on the reclaimed land, Smt. Daisy Lazarus, the owner of the land where the church stands today, decided to donate her property measuring one acre and twenty-five cents for the church building. On the 26th of Meenam, Kollam Era 1100, a title deed (*theeradharam*) was registered as a gift (*inaam*) in favor of the then missionary Rev. Arthur Parker on behalf of the London Missionary Society. That was how this church came to be established at its present location. The first temporary structure erected here collapsed during a fierce storm on a Saturday night in 1926. Yet, it took them only a few hours to construct a fresh shed so that worship could proceed uninterrupted the very next day. The foundation stone of the church where we worship today was laid in 1930 by Sri. Lazarus Contractor, who was then the Church Secretary. Subsequently, permission from the government to construct the church was secured through the dedicated efforts of church minister Sri. Enos Evangelist, Church Secretary Sri. Lazarus Contractor, and the Church Committee members. In 1932, a foundation measuring 60 feet in length and 20 feet in width was built out of granite. Above it, a roof of palm leaves and bamboo was fashioned. On October 16, 1933, the church was dedicated in the name of God by Rev. R. H. Eastaff, M.A., B.D., the LMS missionary of the time. With this, the Aruvikkara parish etched its place in history as a center of hope for an oppressed community. On January 13, 1936, the Church Committee meeting held under the presidency of the then church worker, Sri. Dasan Evangelist, decided to commence church construction works through voluntary labor (*shramadanam*) and donations gathered from well-wishers. It was also resolved to extend the church building by 10 feet and construct wings and vestries. Furthermore, it was decided to request financial aid from the Mission for these works. The Mission only agreed to supply timber for the work from Paruthippally. Undaunted, the congregation raised granite pillars, erected the roof, and gradually built up the walls. A few wooden benches were also constructed during this period. In 1943, during the tenure of Sri. David Samuel Upadeshi, tiled roofing was laid and the interior of the church building was plastered and whitewashed, sponsored as a donation by Sri. Daniel Contractor of Nanthancode, Thiruvananthapuram. It was also during this period that systematic Sunday School and Christian Endeavour (CE) fellowships were organized. Sri. George of Kalkuzhi Prosper House served as the first secretary of the Sunday School. In 1946, during the tenure of Sri. Sarangam Evangelist, construction of the Mission House began under the leadership of the Church Committee and was completed in 1950. Sri. Samuel, the then treasurer, supervised the work. Rev. M. Thompson was the first presbyter to reside in this Mission House. It was during his ministry that floor tiles were laid inside the church. On February 22, 1952, the founding leader Sri. Lazarus Contractor fell asleep in the Lord. Following this, Sri. Johny Deacon of Velloorkonam took charge as Church Secretary and led the committee. The demise of Sri. Lazarus Contractor—who played a decisive role in building up the Church by prayerfully offering a portion of his wealth—was an irreparable loss to the congregation. His role in guiding a socially backward community into mainstream society through the Christian pathway remains commendable. Honoring their father’s last wish and considering the financial stability required for Aruvikkara Church to be elevated to an independent pastorate, his children registered an estate of one acre and eighty-four cents at Kakkodu in the name of Properties Board Chairman Rev. William Brown on behalf of Aruvikkara Church. This property is known as the Lazarus Memorial Compound. Jacob, M.A., B.D., was present on the occasion. Rev. A. Isaac, B.A., B.D., was installed as the first Presbyter.
